Step 1

Prepare the Machine

🔌
Turn off the machine

The display emits light that creates artifacts in the 3D scan. Power it down completely, or place a matte sticker over the screen.

🧹
Clean the plastic casing

Wipe down the glossy surfaces with a microfiber cloth to remove fingerprints and smudges. These create false specular highlights.

💡
Set up diffused lighting

Avoid harsh overhead fluorescent lights. Use window light or multiple soft light sources to minimize hard reflections on the plastic.

🪑
Position on a table

Place the machine on a plain-colored surface (not white — use a gray or beige table). Ensure you can walk a full 360° around it.

💡
Pro Tip — Glossy Plastics

If you have spray-on matte coating (like Helling 3D Anti-Glare Spray), a light mist on the glossiest areas dramatically improves scan quality. It washes off easily.

Step 2

Scan with iPhone 16 Plus

📱 Recommended Apps

Recommended
Polycam

Best for object scanning with LiDAR. Exports directly to USDZ and GLB. Free tier available.

Luma AI

Uses Gaussian Splatting for photorealistic captures. Cloud processing. Great for complex geometry.

Reality Composer

Apple's free app. Good for simple scans and native USDZ export. Built into iOS.

🎥 Scanning Technique

Orbit 1 — Eye Level

Walk slowly around the machine at table height. Keep steady, ~60 cm distance. Full 360°.

Orbit 2 — High Angle (45°)

Raise the phone above the machine, angled downward. Captures the top surface details.

Orbit 3 — Low Angle

Crouch down and capture the underside lip and front panel. Capture connection ports on the back.

⏱️
Timing

Each orbit takes about 30-45 seconds. Total scan time: ~2-3 minutes. Move slowly and steadily — no jerky movements.

Step 3

Process & Export

🔄 From Gaussian Splat to AR-Ready Mesh

Your iPhone captures a Gaussian Splat — a cloud of colored light blobs. This looks amazing in a browser but phones expect solid 3D polygon meshes for AR. The scanning apps convert ("bake") the splat into standard formats:

Apple

.USDZ

For iOS devices. Used by AR Quick Look in Safari. Set this as the ios-src attribute in model-viewer.

Typical size: 5-15 MB
Universal

.GLB

For Android + web viewing. Used by Scene Viewer and model-viewer's main src attribute.

Typical size: 3-10 MB
⚠️
Important — Scale Calibration

Before exporting, ensure the model dimensions match the real machine (46.7 × 38.7 × 19.4 cm). Most scanning apps let you set a reference measurement. This ensures 1:1 scale in AR.

Step 4

Deploy to This App

A
Replace the placeholder model

Copy your exported files to the project's public/models/ folder:

public/models/apd-machine.glb   ← Android + Web
public/models/apd-machine.usdz  ← iOS AR Quick Look
B
Update hotspot positions

Open the viewer page, use browser DevTools to adjust data-position and data-normal attributes on each hotspot to match your real model's geometry.

C
Deploy to web hosting

Push to Vercel or Netlify for instant public URL:

npx vercel --prod

Share the URL or generated QR code with patients.
