🥗 Nutrition

Fueling Your Body on PD

PD is a daily treatment, which means your body stays more balanced. This allows for a more flexible diet compared to hemodialysis.

Home › Diet & Fluids

🥩 Protein: The #1 Priority

In PD, you lose a small amount of protein through the dialysate during every exchange. This means you need MORE protein than before you started dialysis.

  • Goal: High-quality protein at every meal (lean meats, fish, poultry, eggs, Tofu).
  • Signs of low protein: Muscle loss, slow wound healing, and higher infection risk.
  • Dietary Tip: If you struggle to eat enough, talk to your dietitian about protein supplements specifically for dialysis.

⚖️ Potassium & Phosphorus

🍌 Potassium (Often More Liberal)

Because PD happens daily, most patients can eat moderate amounts of potassium-rich foods like bananas, potatoes, and tomatoes. However, your blood levels are the final guide.

🧀 Phosphorus (Still Restricted)

Phosphorus is harder to remove with dialysis. Too much causes itchy skin and brittle bones. Avoid: Dark colas, processed cheeses, beer, and nuts.

💧 Sodium & Fluid Balance

Your "Dry Weight" is your body weight without excess fluid. Salt (sodium) makes you thirsty and holds onto water.

  • Sodium limit: Usually < 2,000mg per day. Avoid processed, canned, and fast foods.
  • Fluid limit: Depends on how much you still urinate. Typical limit is 1,000ml to 1,500ml total intake per day.
  • Managing Thirst: Try sucking on ice chips, using small glasses, or chewing sugar-free gum.
⚠️

Dextrose & Weight: PD solution contains dextrose (sugar), which your body absorbs. This adds calories. Be mindful of weight gain and blood sugar levels if you have diabetes.

🥘 Smart Food Choices

Food Group Choose More Choose Less
Breads/Grains White bread, rice, pasta, couscous Whole grains (higher phosphorus)
Vegetables Carrots, cucumbers, green beans, cabbage Spinach, beets, swiss chard
Beverages Water, clear sodas, apple/cranberry juice Colas, iced tea, orange juice
Seasoning Fresh herbs, lemon, garlic, pepper Salt, soy sauce, garlic salt

Consult a Specialist

A renal dietitian is a key member of your care team. They will create a meal plan tailored to your blood results.
