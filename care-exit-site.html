🩹 Self-Care

Exit Site Care

Your exit site is a window into your body. Keeping it clean and dry is the first line of defense against infection.

Home › Exit Site Care

🧼 Daily Cleaning Routine

You should clean your exit site every day, either after showering or as its own dedicated routine.

Wash Hands First
Wash hands thoroughly with soap. Put on your mask.
Remove Old Dressing
Gently remove the old tape and gauze. Stop if you feel pain. Moisten with sterile saline if stuck.
Inspect
Look closely at the skin around the tube. Is it red? Is there fluid? Is it tender?
Clean & Dry
Clean in a circular motion starting at the tube and moving outward. Use the soap or solution recommended by your nurse. Pat dry with sterile gauze.
Apply Dressing
Apply prescribed antibiotic cream (if ordered) and a fresh sterile dressing. Tape the catheter securely to prevent pulling.

🚦 Grading Your Exit Site

Learn to recognize what is normal and what needs a call to the clinic.

✅ Healthy Site

  • Skin color is normal (no redness)
  • No pain or tenderness
  • No drainage or fluid
  • Skin is firm around the exit

⚠️ Infected Site

  • Redness extending >1cm from tube
  • Pus (yellow, green, or thick white fluid)
  • Swelling or "fullness"
  • Warmth or tenderness to touch

⚓ Anchoring Your Catheter

"Tugging" on the catheter is a common cause of exit site irritation. It damages the delicate new skin forming inside the tunnel.

  • Use a "Stress Loop": Tape the catheter in a small loop to your skin. If the tube is pulled, the tape takes the strain, not your exit site.
  • PD Belts & Pouches: Many patients find dedicated belts more comfortable than tape for long-term wear.
  • Nighttime: Ensure the tube has enough slack to allow you to turn in bed without pulling.

Stay Vigilant

Prevention is always better than cure. Good hygiene and careful monitoring are your best tools.
