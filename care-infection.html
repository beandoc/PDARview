🔬 What is Peritonitis?

Peritonitis is an inflammation or infection of the lining of your abdominal cavity (the peritoneum). In PD patients, it usually happens when bacteria enter through the catheter or connections.

💡

The Golden Rule: If your drain fluid is CLOUDY, assume it is peritonitis until proven otherwise by your nurse. Do not wait for pain or fever to start.

🛡️ The 3 Pillars of Prevention

🧼

Hand Hygiene

2-minute hand wash with antibacterial soap before every setup and connection. No exceptions.

🔗

No-Touch Tech

Never touch the sterile tips of your catheter or tubing. Keep your mask on to prevent germs from your breath.

🩹

Site Care

Keep your exit site dry and protected. Use anchoring to prevent tugging and trauma to the skin.

⚠️ Identifying High-Risk Situations

Be extra vigilant during these times:

  • Constipation: Bacteria can migrate from the bowel into the peritoneum. Ensure you have regular bowel movements.
  • Colds/Flu: Coughing or sneezing can spray germs. Always wear your mask.
  • Pets: Pet dander and hair are major sources of bacteria. Keep them away from dialysis supplies and your workspace.
  • Touching the floor: If your transfer set or tubing drops on the floor, it is contaminated.

🏃 Action Plan: If You Suspect Infection

If you see cloudy fluid or have abdominal pain:

Stop & Clamp
Finish your drain if possible, but do not start a new fill.
Save the Bag
Do not throw away the cloudy drain bag. Clamp the tubing to keep the sample inside.
Call Immediately
Call your PD nurse. They will ask you to bring the bag in or go to the ER.
Begin Antibiotics
Most peritonitis is treated by adding antibiotics directly into your dialysate bags.
