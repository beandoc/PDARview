Recognizing Emergencies

Knowing when to call for help is a vital part of your PD training. Stay calm—most issues can be managed quickly if caught early.

Home › Emergencies

🦠 Peritonitis: The #1 Concern

Peritonitis is an infection inside your abdomen. It is serious but treatable with antibiotics if found early.

Warning Signs (Call Nurse Immediately):

  • Cloudy drain fluid (instead of clear/yellow). Even slightly cloudy is a concern.
  • Abdominal pain — sharp, constant, or crampy.
  • Fever or chills.
  • Nausea or vomiting.
🛑

If your drain fluid is cloudy, SAVE THE BAG. Your nurse will need to test the fluid to identify the bacteria and choose the right antibiotic.

🩸 Blood in the Fluid

Seeing red or pink in your drain fluid can be scary, but it's often not an emergency.

  • Minor pink tint: Often due to heavy lifting, menstruation, or minor irritation. Call your nurse during business hours.
  • Bright red "Tomato Soup" fluid: This is more serious. Call your nurse immediately or go to the ER.

✂️ Catheter Damage

If your catheter or transfer set gets a cut, leak, or hole:

  1. Clamp it immediately between your body and the leak. Use a smooth clamp (not a binder clip or clothes pin if possible, but use anything in an absolute emergency).
  2. Do not connect to the machine.
  3. Call your nurse immediately to have the tubing replaced sterilely.

🫁 Breathing & Swelling

This relates to fluid overload — having too much water in your body.

  • Shortness of breath (especially while sitting or lying flat).
  • Sudden high blood pressure (much higher than your normal).
  • Severe swelling in face or hands.
⚠️

Call your doctor or go to the ER if you are struggling to breathe. This may mean fluid is building up in your lungs.

📞 Your Emergency Contacts

Keep these numbers programmable in your phone and on your fridge.

Contact Reason to Call
PD Nurse (24/7) Cloudy fluid, catheter damage, fever, pain, machine failure
Nephrologist Chronic symptoms, medication questions, diet concerns
Supply Company Missing or damaged equipment, ordering bags
Emergency (911) Chest pain, severe difficulty breathing, loss of consciousness

Stay Safe & Prepared

Regular self-monitoring catches most of these issues before they become emergencies.
