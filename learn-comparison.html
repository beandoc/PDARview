PD vs Hemodialysis

Both types of dialysis save lives. Understanding the differences helps you choose what fits your lifestyle best.

Home › PD vs Hemodialysis

🔄 Two Ways to Replace Kidney Function

When your kidneys can no longer filter your blood effectively, dialysis takes over that job. There are two main types, each with its own approach:

  • Peritoneal Dialysis (PD) — uses the lining of your abdomen (peritoneum) as a filter. Done at home, usually overnight with a machine.
  • Hemodialysis (HD) — uses an external machine to pump blood through a filter. Typically done at a dialysis centre 3 times per week.

Neither is "better" in all situations — the right choice depends on your medical condition, lifestyle, and personal preferences. Many patients do well on either modality.

📊 Side-by-Side Comparison

💡 Pros & Cons at a Glance

Peritoneal Dialysis

✅ Pros

  • Home-based — no travel to centre
  • No needles required
  • More dietary freedom
  • Continuous gentle dialysis
  • Daytime freedom (APD)
  • Better for residual kidney function

⚠️ Cons

  • Daily self-care commitment required
  • Risk of peritonitis if technique is poor
  • Supply storage at home
  • Abdominal fullness during dwells
  • Not suitable for all body types

Hemodialysis

✅ Pros

  • Nurse-managed at centre — less self-responsibility
  • Social environment with other patients
  • Faster per-session waste removal
  • No supplies stored at home

⚠️ Cons

  • 3 trips to centre per week
  • Needles each session
  • Strict diet and fluid limits
  • Post-dialysis fatigue ("washout")
  • Blood pressure swings during treatment

🤔 Which Is Right for Me?

Consider PD if you value:

  • Independence and home-based treatment
  • Minimal disruption to work, school, or family life
  • Avoiding needles
  • A more flexible diet
  • Travel flexibility

Consider HD if you prefer:

  • Nurse-managed treatment with professional oversight
  • No daily self-care routine at home
  • Social interaction at the dialysis centre
  • Have abdominal surgery history that limits PD
💬

Talk to your nephrologist. The best dialysis modality is the one that works for YOUR life. Many patients start on PD and can always switch to HD later if needed — and vice versa. There's no wrong first choice.
