🕐 A Typical Day on APD

If you're on Automated Peritoneal Dialysis (APD), your machine works while you sleep. Here's what most patients' days look like:

🌅 6:30 – 7:00 AM
Disconnect from the Machine
The cycler has finished your overnight exchanges. You disconnect the tubing, cap your transfer set using sterile technique, and secure your catheter. The whole process takes about 5–10 minutes.
🩹 7:00 – 7:15 AM
Morning Self-Check
Record your weight, check blood pressure, and inspect your exit site. Look at the drain bag — the fluid should be clear or pale yellow. Note tonight's drain volume in your log.
☀️ 7:30 AM – 5:00 PM
Your Day Is Yours
Work, school, errands, hobbies — your day is completely normal. The last fill ("day dwell") sits quietly in your abdomen, slowly cleaning your blood until evening. Most patients don't feel it at all.
🍽️ 6:00 PM
Dinner & Medications
Enjoy a balanced meal following your dietary guidelines. Take your phosphate binders with food, and any other prescribed medications. Your PD nurse will give you a personalized diet plan.
🧤 9:30 PM
Set Up for Tonight's Treatment
Wash your hands thoroughly (2 minutes with soap), prepare your workspace, and set up the cycler. Load the cassette, connect solution bags, and prime the tubing. Total setup: ~15 minutes once you're practiced.
🌙 10:00 PM
Connect & Sleep
Connect your transfer set to the machine tubing using sterile technique. Press Start. Get into bed. The machine runs quietly for 8–10 hours, performing 4–6 exchanges while you sleep.

📦 Managing Your Supplies

PD requires regular supplies delivered to your home. Here's what to expect:

  • Monthly delivery: Boxes of dialysate solution bags (typically 30–60 bags/month depending on prescription)
  • Storage space: You'll need a clean, dry area — a spare closet, garage shelf, or dedicated corner works well
  • Each box weighs ~8–10 kg — most patients stack them on shelving units
  • Cassettes & tubing: Delivered with your solution supply
  • Exit site supplies: Cleaning solution, dressings, caps — delivered monthly
🚚

Delivery is handled for you. Your PD supplier (e.g., Baxter) coordinates monthly home deliveries. You can adjust delivery dates, quantities, and even arrange supplies to be sent to holiday destinations.

🏃 Exercise & Physical Activity

Exercise is not only safe on PD — it's encouraged. Staying active helps control blood pressure, improves energy, and supports mental health.

  • Walking, cycling, swimming (once exit site is fully healed, with waterproof dressing)
  • Yoga and stretching — excellent for flexibility and stress relief
  • Light strength training — avoid excessive abdominal strain in the first few months
  • During dwell: Most patients exercise comfortably with solution in the abdomen. Drain before intense exercise if you feel fullness.
⚠️

Avoid: Contact sports (risk of catheter injury), heavy lifting (>10 kg in the early months), and immersing your exit site in untreated water (lakes, pools without proper protection).

✈️ Travelling on PD

Many PD patients travel regularly — even internationally. Here's how:

  • Domestic travel: Your supplier ships supplies to your destination 1–2 weeks ahead
  • International travel: Your PD clinic coordinates with the supplier's international network
  • Air travel: Carry one night's supplies as cabin luggage; get a medical letter from your doctor
  • Hotels: Request a room with a table next to the bed and a nearby power outlet
  • Camping: CAPD (manual exchanges) works well for short trips without electricity
💡

Plan 6–8 weeks ahead for international trips. Your PD nurse can help coordinate supplies. Many patients travel to destinations across Europe, Asia, and North America without issues.

💼 Work, School & Social Life

One of the biggest advantages of APD is that your treatment happens overnight, leaving your days free:

  • Full-time work: Most APD patients work full-time without any accommodation
  • Students: Attend school normally — no missed classes for dialysis
  • Social life: Evening plans? Start your machine a bit later. One late night won't harm you — just make sure you get your full treatment most nights
  • Eating out: You can eat out — just be mindful of phosphorus and sodium
  • Telling others: It's entirely your choice how much to share. Many patients find that explaining PD to close friends and family builds a support network

🧠 Your Emotional Well-Being

Starting dialysis is a major life change. It's completely normal to feel anxious, frustrated, or overwhelmed — especially in the first few weeks. Here are some things that help:

  • Routine: Once PD becomes habit (usually within 2–4 weeks), it feels much easier
  • Support groups: Connect with other PD patients — online communities and local hospital groups
  • Talk to someone: Ask your care team about counselling if you're struggling
  • Celebrate milestones: Your first successful night on the machine, first trouble-free month, first trip — they matter
  • Stay active: Exercise and social activity are proven mood boosters
💚

You are not alone. Millions of people worldwide live full, active lives on PD. The first month is the hardest — it gets easier, and your PD nurse is always a phone call away.
