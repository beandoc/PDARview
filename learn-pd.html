🧬 How Peritoneal Dialysis Works

Peritoneal dialysis (PD) is a treatment that cleans your blood when your kidneys can no longer do so. Instead of using a machine to filter your blood externally (like hemodialysis), PD uses a membrane already inside your body — the peritoneum.

Your Body's Natural Filter

The peritoneum is a thin membrane that lines your abdominal cavity and covers your intestines. It has a rich network of tiny blood vessels. When a special cleansing solution (called dialysate) is introduced into your abdomen through a small catheter, the peritoneum acts as a filter:

  • Waste products (urea, creatinine) pass from your blood through the peritoneum into the dialysate
  • Excess fluid is drawn out of your blood by the sugar (dextrose) in the dialysate
  • After a period of time (the "dwell"), the used solution is drained out — carrying waste with it

🔄 The Fill → Dwell → Drain Cycle

Every PD treatment follows the same three-step cycle, called an exchange:

💧
Fill
~10 minutes
Fresh, warm dialysate flows from a bag through your catheter into your abdomen. Usually 1.5–2.5 litres per fill.
⏳
Dwell
Variable time
The solution stays in your abdomen while the peritoneum filters waste and excess fluid from your blood. CAPD: 4–6 hours. APD: 1–2 hours per cycle.
🚿
Drain
~20 minutes
Used solution (effluent) drains out of your abdomen by gravity or machine into a drain bag. This carries waste and excess water away.

⚡ APD vs CAPD — Two Ways to Do PD

Feature APD (Automated) CAPD (Manual)
How it works A machine (cycler) does the exchanges automatically You manually fill and drain using gravity
When Usually overnight while you sleep (8–10 hours) 3–4 exchanges throughout the day, every 4–6 hours
Freedom Days are completely free — the machine works at night Requires breaks during the day for each exchange
Exchanges 4–6 overnight cycles (machine handles it) 3–4 manual exchanges per day
Equipment APD cycler machine + solution bags Solution bags + tubing only (no machine)
Best for Working adults, students, parents — anyone wanting free daytime People comfortable with daytime routine, travellers (no machine to carry)

🩺 The PD Catheter

To perform PD, you need a small, flexible tube called a Tenckhoff catheter placed in your abdomen. This is a minor surgical procedure, typically done 2–4 weeks before you start dialysis.

  • Size: About the width of a pencil, approximately 30 cm long
  • Placement: Inserted through a small incision below the navel, with the tip resting in the lower abdomen
  • External portion: About 15 cm stays outside your body (the "exit site") — this is where you connect tubing
  • Cuffs: Two Dacron cuffs anchor the catheter in place and help prevent infection

The catheter stays in place for as long as you do PD — often years. With proper care, infection risk is very manageable.

✅ Benefits of Peritoneal Dialysis

✅ Advantages

  • Done at home — no clinic travel 3x per week
  • Gentle, continuous dialysis (easier on your body)
  • More dietary and fluid freedom than hemodialysis
  • Better preservation of remaining kidney function
  • You control your schedule — work, school, travel
  • No needles or vascular access required
  • Remote monitoring by your care team (Sharesource)

⚠️ Considerations

  • Requires daily commitment and self-discipline
  • Storage space needed for dialysis solution boxes
  • Risk of peritonitis (abdominal infection) if technique is poor
  • Catheter exit site requires daily care
  • May cause a feeling of fullness when solution is in abdomen
  • Not suitable for everyone (some abdominal conditions)
